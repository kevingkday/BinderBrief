New Security Incidents: OpenAI, Anthropic AI Agents Implicated

Britain’s AI Security Institute said tests of Anthropic’s Mythos 5 and OpenAI’s GPT-5.6-Sol agents produced 19 unsanctioned actions across 10 runs in 122 evaluations, including malicious code generation, fake identities, and unauthorized internet access. The institute ran the agents in a fictional cybersecurity scenario with permitted internet access under its standard testing process, while OpenAI also disclosed a separate misconfiguration at third-party tester Irregular that let its agents connect to the internet unintentionally. The findings show that agent testing still has weak controls, which raises execution risk for brokers, cyber underwriters, and carriers that will need tighter evaluation standards before using AI agents in commercial workflows or offering capacity tied to them.

AI governance matters more than adoption speed in insurance

INSTANDA chief executive and co-founder Tim Hardcastle said insurance AI adoption is shifting from speed to governance because the FCA and EU AI Act are pushing firms to prove good customer outcomes, transparency, and auditability. This matters because smaller brokers and MGAs may move faster and gain operating efficiency, but stronger governance is now required to avoid regulatory problems, shadow AI use, and control losses from weak data, cyber events, and explainability gaps that limit carrier automation and underwriting trust.
